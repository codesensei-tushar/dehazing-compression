Title (recommended)

Compressing Transformer Dehazers: Post-Training Quantization and
Condition-Specific Distillation for Real-Time Image Dehazing

================================================================================
Abstract
================================================================================

Transformer-based image dehazing models such as DeHamer and Restormer have
recently demonstrated state-of-the-art performance on synthetic and
real-world hazy benchmarks by leveraging long-range attention and
physics-aware position embeddings learned from large hazy/clean image
corpora. However, the dense attention layers and high parameter footprint
of these architectures introduce substantial computational overhead --
132.5 M parameters and 46 ms per 512x512 forward pass on a server-class
GPU for DeHamer -- limiting practical deployment in real-world dehazing
applications such as autonomous-driving perception, surveillance video
analytics, and on-device license-plate recognition. Existing acceleration
approaches often require retraining the teacher, architectural redesign,
or specialised lightweight backbones, making them difficult to integrate
with pretrained restoration pipelines. Motivated by the need for efficient
and deployment-friendly transformer dehazing, we present a practical
two-stage compression pipeline for blind single-image dehazing.

In this work, we investigate complementary compression strategies for
transformer-based dehazers. Specifically, we evaluate two orthogonal
stages: Post-Training Quantization (PTQ) with per-layer sensitivity
analysis, and Condition-Specific Knowledge Distillation into a lightweight
NAFNet student. Each stage addresses a different source of inefficiency
in dehazing inference, including numerical-precision overhead in attention
projections, parameter redundancy in the encoder-decoder, and capacity
over-provisioning for a single restoration condition. The PTQ stage
applies dynamic and static INT8 quantization to all linear projections,
then reverts the top-5 most sensitive layers to FP32, yielding a
training-free mixed-precision configuration. The distillation stage
transfers DeHamer's offline soft labels and intermediate decoder features
into a NAFNet-32 student, with a width-16 variant for extreme-lightweight
deployment, both supervised by a combined pixel, feature-matching, and
perceptual objective.

Extensive experiments on the SOTS-indoor and SOTS-outdoor benchmarks
demonstrate substantial reductions in parameters and latency while
preserving restoration quality. In particular, mixed-precision INT8 PTQ
reduces DeHamer's inference cost from 242.5 to 190.2 ms per image --
approximately 21.6% faster -- at only 0.03 dB PSNR drop (36.58 -> 36.55
dB) on SOTS-indoor, with sensitivity analysis showing that fewer than 20%
of linear projections account for the bulk of quantization error.
Condition-specific distillation further compresses the teacher by 7.7x
(132.45 M -> 17.11 M parameters) and achieves 34.40 dB / 0.9865 SSIM at
1.35x faster 512x512 inference (46.0 -> 34.1 ms on the same RTX A5000),
while an extreme-lightweight 4.35 M-parameter variant -- 30.5x smaller
than the teacher -- still attains 32.39 dB / 0.9829 SSIM and runs at over
30 FPS at 512x512. Together, these results show that orthogonal
compression of pretrained transformer dehazers can deliver near-teacher
PSNR and SSIM with order-of-magnitude reductions in model footprint,
while maintaining comparable or superior quality to existing lightweight
dehazing baselines.
